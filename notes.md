# Pitch script: Every car is an ad space (3 minutes)

Spoken text in normal type, **[stage directions in brackets]**. About 330 spoken words: at a calm pace that leaves
roughly 40 seconds for the live demo moments (the car speaking, cards appearing).

---

## 0:00 – 0:30 · Hook

**[Stand next to the car. Projector off, rear screen black.]**

Every year, 2.5 million new cars roll out of BMW's factories.

Every one of them carries about two square metres of surface that shows… nothing.

**[Pause.]**

What if all of that space could talk — to the driver, and to everyone around?

Here's the journey of a BMW driver, in the next generation of AI-powered cars.

---

## 0:30 – 2:15 · The journey

**[Start the demo: projector on, rear screen on.]**

**1 · The traffic light**

Green light. The car in front doesn't move. Our driver is not happy.

**[Driver, loud, in German:]** "Hey, grüner wird's nicht, jetzt fahr doch endlich, du Penner!"

**[The car speaks, friendly:]** "Es ist grün, würden Sie bitte losfahren? Danke!"

Road rage in, politeness out — in under a second.

**2 · "I'm hungry"**

**[Driver:]** "Boah, hab ich Hunger."

**[A McDonald's card appears on the road in front of the car: turn left, 250 m.]**

The car listened — and the next restaurant appears on the road. Not because of cookies, but because of what's
happening in the car right now. That's the most relevant ad there is.

**3 · People in front of the car**

**[Someone walks in front of the car.]**

The car sees the pedestrian, and the projected path turns red. Ad space and safety, in one.

**4 · Who's behind us?**

**[Point to the rear screen: the number-plate job card.]**

Behind us, a Porsche. The rear camera recognises it — so the rear window shows the right ad for a Porsche
driver: a job pitch. Every road user gets their own message.

**5 · The traffic jam**

**[Driver:]** "Lass uns Pacman spielen."

**[The game appears on the road.]**

Stuck in traffic? Now the road is a game. Entertainment is ad space too.

---

## 2:15 – 2:45 · Business model

So who pays for this?

Advertisers. They book by context: what people talk about, where the car is, who drives behind it.

The driver gets a share — or a cheaper car, with ad-funded leasing.

And BMW gets recurring revenue for the whole life of every car — with just a projector and a screen.

And what is all this worth?

A square metre of billboard costs about 16 euros a day. 2.5 million cars, two square metres each…

---

## 2:45 – 3:00 · Close

**[Projector shows "Your ad here" on the road.]**

…that's **80 million euros. Per day.**

And it's already out there, driving around, unused.

We turn every car into a medium. Thank you.

## Before the pitch

- **Check the numbers and have a source ready:** BMW's yearly deliveries (about 2.5 million), €16 per m² of
  billboard per day, 2 m² per car. Say "about" — the jury will ask.
- **Questions to expect, with a one-line answer each:**
  - *Privacy?* — Nothing is recorded or stored; the demo uses a cloud model for speech, a product would run it in
    the car. Cameras only detect, they do not identify anyone.
  - *Distraction?* — Ads for the driver only when the car stands or on request; projections stay on the ground.
  - *Is projecting onto public roads allowed?* — Not yet everywhere: today a concept, rollout where regulation
    allows (e.g. private grounds, parking, drive-throughs first).
- **Demo backup:** if the car or the network fails, `./start.sh sim` runs everything on the recorded drive, or show
  a video of the journey.
- **Roles:** one narrator, one "driver" for the lines in the car, one person to walk in front of the car, one in a
  suit (or a Porsche photo) behind it for the job pitch.
- **The car's lines are German on purpose:** that is what the speech demo understands and answers in. Rehearse
  them once with `./start.sh` running.
- **Leave out the flirt mode** in front of the jury.
